🧮 How the numbers work

  • Hourly rate: a salary is divided by the hours you are paid for in a year, hours per week × paid weeks per year. The default 40 hours × 52 weeks makes 2,080 hours, the full-time year used in U.S. wage statistics. A biweekly paycheck is two weeks of pay, and a monthly amount is a twelfth of the year.
  • Per second and per minute: the hourly rate divided by 3,600 and by 60.
  • Per workday: weekly pay divided by your workdays per week. Per month: yearly pay divided by 12. That is an average, because months have different numbers of workdays.
  • Live ticker: the time on the clock multiplied by your pay per second. It reads your device clock instead of counting ticks, so it stays accurate if your screen sleeps or you switch tabs. Pause stops the clock. Changing your pay keeps what you have earned and uses the new rate from then on.
  • Before taxes: every figure is gross pay. Taxes and deductions are not taken out.

Source for the 2,080-hour full-time year: Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics technical notes (a U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics program, as published by the New York State Department of Labor). Checked September 30, 2026.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How much do I make per second?

Divide your hourly pay by 3,600, the number of seconds in an hour. For a salary, first divide it by the hours you are paid for in a year: 2,080 for 40 hours a week and 52 weeks. For example, $52,000 a year is $25 an hour, about $0.42 a minute and $0.0069 a second.

How many work hours are in a year?

A full-time year is usually counted as 2,080 hours: 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, with paid holidays and vacation included. The Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics survey run by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and state labor departments uses the same 2,080-hour year. If your schedule is different, change the hours per week or paid weeks per year.

Is this before or after taxes?

Before taxes. The ticker uses the gross pay you enter. Your take-home pay is lower after income taxes, Social Security and Medicare taxes, and any deductions for benefits or retirement savings.

Can it count breaks, overtime or a shift that already started?

Press Pause during an unpaid break and Resume when you are back. If you earn a higher overtime rate, change your pay to that rate while you work it: the ticker keeps what you have already earned and uses the new rate from that moment. If you forgot to press Start, enter the time you started work and press Count from this time.

Is my salary saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser and nothing is saved, so the numbers are gone when you close the page. Your pay goes into a link only when you press Copy result or Share link, so the person who opens the link sees the same result.
